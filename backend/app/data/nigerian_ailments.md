# Medical Guidelines for Common Ailments

## Malaria
**Overview:** A life-threatening disease spread to humans by some types of mosquitoes. Mostly found in tropical countries like Nigeria.
**Common Symptoms:** High fever, profuse sweating, headache, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea, anemia, muscle pain, convulsions, coma, and bloody stools.
**Red Flags (Emergency):** Impaired consciousness, severe anemia, respiratory distress (difficulty breathing), multiple convulsions, deep coma.
**Recommended Specialist:** General Practitioner, Infectious Disease Specialist. If Red Flags are present, direct immediately to the Emergency Room.

## Typhoid Fever
**Overview:** A bacterial infection that can lead to a high fever, diarrhea, and vomiting. It is typically transmitted through contaminated food and drinking water.
**Common Symptoms:** Sustained high fever (up to 40°C or 104°F), weakness, stomach pain, headache, diarrhea or constipation, cough, loss of appetite, and a rash of flat, rose-colored spots.
**Red Flags (Emergency):** Severe abdominal pain (could indicate intestinal perforation), severe vomiting, dehydration, shock.
**Recommended Specialist:** General Practitioner, Gastroenterologist.

## Cholera
**Overview:** An acute diarrheal illness caused by infection of the intestine with Vibrio cholerae bacteria.
**Common Symptoms:** Severe watery diarrhea, vomiting, leg cramps, rapid loss of body fluids leading to dehydration and shock.
**Red Flags (Emergency):** Severe dehydration (sunken eyes, dry mouth, extreme thirst), lethargy, weak pulse, low blood pressure. Cholera is almost always an emergency due to rapid dehydration.
**Recommended Specialist:** Emergency Medicine, General Practitioner (Urgent Care).

## Peptic Ulcer Disease
**Overview:** Open sores that develop on the inside lining of your stomach and the upper portion of your small intestine.
**Common Symptoms:** Burning stomach pain, feeling of fullness, bloating or belching, intolerance to fatty foods, heartburn, nausea.
**Red Flags (Emergency):** Vomiting blood (which may appear red or black), dark blood in stools, or stools that are black or tarry, trouble breathing, feeling faint, unexplained weight loss, severe, sharp stomach pain.
**Recommended Specialist:** Gastroenterologist.

## Lassa Fever
**Overview:** An acute viral hemorrhagic illness endemic in parts of West Africa, including Nigeria.
**Common Symptoms:** Gradual onset of fever, general weakness, and malaise. After a few days, headache, sore throat, muscle pain, chest pain, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, cough, and abdominal pain may follow.
**Red Flags (Emergency):** Facial swelling, fluid in the lung cavity, bleeding from the mouth, nose, vagina or gastrointestinal tract, and low blood pressure.
**Recommended Specialist:** Infectious Disease Specialist, Emergency Room.
